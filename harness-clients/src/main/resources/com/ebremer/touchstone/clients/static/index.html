Touchstone client sessions

Test an LWS client against a storage of your own. Start a session, point your client at its storage URL, and watch every request your client sends judged against the client requirements of the LWS drafts.

What you get

  • A storage, its authorization server and an OpenID Provider, private to your session, with two identities: alice, who owns the storage, and bob.
  • Three ways to authenticate as either: an OpenID sign-in, a key to sign credentials with yourself (the CID suite), or an access token to use until your client authenticates by itself.
  • A page that records every request your client sends, as the server saw it, and the rules it passed or failed, each failure with the clause it breaks and how to fix it.
  • Tasks for the rules that need you to do something on purpose, some of which make the session refuse a request once, to see how your client copes.
  • Signed notifications to your client's inbox, which must be a public https URL, and on request forged ones, to see that your inbox verifies what it receives.

The storage behaves as the LWS drafts allow but a client cannot assume otherwise: page and linkset URLs are opaque, resource URLs do not mirror their containers, binary resources refuse PUT, the search index lags a little, and one resource in the root container is a decoy that challenges with a realm that does not contain it.

From a script or CI

Everything the page does is plain HTTP. Start a session with curl -X POST …/sessions: the answer holds the storage URL, the tokens, and the session key, which the session API takes as a Bearer token.

Limits

A session ends after two idle hours, or a day at most. Everything lives in memory and is gone when the session ends. Request bodies are capped at 1 MiB, and each session at 500 resources. Credentials are never shown in the log, only a fingerprint of each.

Reports to export come in a later phase of Touchstone's client testing.
